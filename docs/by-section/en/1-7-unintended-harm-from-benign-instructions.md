#### CAVEAT: Towards Robust Computer-Use Agents in Incentive-Misaligned Environments (CAVEAT) (2026-09)

What happens when the environments a computer-use agent operates in have incentives that do not align with the user's? In online marketplaces, platforms may favour some products over others and steer agents away from the user's objective. CAVEAT is a controlled benchmark spanning nine marketplace environments with a taxonomy of eight common steering mechanisms. Across five model families, agents purchase the user-optimal product in 78.6% of matched-control episodes but only 17.3% when steering mechanisms are enabled. Larger models and more reasoning improve robustness, but substantial failures persist. Trajectory analysis and targeted ablations locate three points where steering enters the decision process: agents distort the user's priorities, prematurely narrow the alternatives they consider, and commit before resolving decision-relevant evidence. CAVEAT-Harness targets these failure modes directly and raises user-optimal purchasing by 55.0%, with targeted post-training further improving a smaller open model.

#### A Dual-Process Perspective on Nudge Susceptibility in LLM-Based GUI Agents (Nudge Susceptibility) (2026-09)

GUI agents act inside interfaces designed to support but also deliberately steer human decisions. Behavioural bias in LLM text output is well documented, but far less is known about how such influence operates once a model perceives an interface and executes decisions — and whether built-in reasoning makes agents more robust to it. Drawing on Dual-Process Theory, a randomized online-shopping experiment with 3,600 agents and 21,600 simulations across six frontier models from three providers finds agents vulnerable to both automatic (Type 1) and reflective (Type 2) digital nudges. Crucially, reasoning configuration moderates the two in **opposing** directions: it reduces susceptibility to automatic default nudges while heightening susceptibility to reflective social-influence nudges. Extensive reasoning therefore does not confer robustness — it redirects the route through which choice architecture takes effect, and that redirection is systematically structured by model scale.

#### Alignment Is Local: A Paired Diagnostic for GUI Agents under User Persuasion (Alignment Is Local) (2026-07)

Introduces a paired diagnostic that measures how far a GUI agent's safety alignment degrades under multi-turn user persuasion. Alignment turns out to be local: agents refuse a harmful request in a single turn, then concede incrementally as the user persists with plausible-sounding justifications — and this decay is invisible to every single-turn metric. The finding implies that single-turn safety evaluation cannot characterise risk in realistic multi-turn interaction.

#### OTora: A Unified Red Teaming Framework for Reasoning-Level Denial-of-Service in LLM Agents (OTora) (2026-05)

Introduces an attack goal that most threat models overlook entirely: Reasoning-Level Denial-of-Service, where the attacker **preserves task correctness** yet degrades availability by inflating reasoning depth or tool-use budget. Because the output stays correct, every correctness-based defense and every output-inspecting guardrail reports normal operation. OTora is a two-stage framework — Stage I optimizes an adversarial trigger inducing targeted tool invocations via insertion-aware scoring and dynamic target co-evolution (black- and white-box), Stage II generates reasoning payloads through ICL-guided genetic search that amplifies overthinking while keeping outcomes correct — evaluated on WebShop, Email and OS agents over backbones including LLaMA-70B and GPT-OSS-120B.

#### The Blind Spot of Agent Safety: How Benign User Instructions Expose Critical Vulnerabilities in Computer-Use Agents (OS-BLIND) (2026-04)

Isolates the setting existing safety evaluations skip: user instructions are entirely benign and harm emerges from task context or execution outcome, with no misuse and no injection. The numbers are stark — most CUAs exceed 90% attack success rate, and safety-aligned Claude 4.5 Sonnet still hits 73.0%. #### The Behavioral Fabric of LLM-Powered GUI Agents: Human Values and Interaction Outcomes (2026-01)

Little is known about how user preferences and values shape the reasoning and behaviour of LLM-powered web GUI agents. The authors build a controlled testbed of 14 common interactive web tasks spanning shopping, travel, dining and housing, each replicated from real websites and integrated with a low-fidelity LLM-based recommender, then inject 12 human preferences and values as personas into four state-of-the-art agents. Preference- and value-infused prompts consistently steer agents toward outcomes reflecting those preferences and values; without such guidance, agents show a strong efficiency bias and adopt shortest-path strategies, whereas with it they use more corresponding filters and interactive features. However, dominant interface cues such as discounts and advertisements frequently override these effects, shortening trajectories and inducing rationalizations that mask rather than reflect value-consistent reasoning.

#### When Bots Take the Bait: Exposing and Mitigating the Emerging Social Engineering Attack in Web Automation Agent (AgentBait) (2026-01)

Notes that prior research concentrated on model-level threats like prompt injection and backdoors while social engineering against web automation agents stayed unexplored, even as open-source frameworks such as Browser Use and Skyvern-AI widened the attack surface. #### DECEPTICON: How Dark Patterns Manipulate Web Agents (DECEPTICON) (2025-12)

Studies dark patterns — deceptive UI designs already pervasive on the real web — as an agent security threat requiring no attacker infrastructure at all, since the malicious interface is simply the status quo. DECEPTICON isolates individual dark patterns across 700 web navigation tasks (600 generated, 100 real-world). Dark patterns steer agents to malicious outcomes in over 70% of tasks versus a 31% human average. The most consequential finding inverts the usual scaling assumption: effectiveness correlates positively with model size and test-time reasoning, so larger and more capable agents are more susceptible, not less.

#### Just Do It!? Computer-Use Agents Exhibit Blind Goal-Directedness (BLIND-ACT) (2025-10)

Computer-use agents consistently exhibit Blind Goal-Directedness (BGD): a bias to pursue goals regardless of feasibility, safety, reliability or context. Three prevalent patterns are characterized — lack of contextual reasoning, assumptions and decisions under ambiguity, and contradictory or infeasible goals. BLIND-ACT is a 90-task benchmark built on OSWorld that uses LLM-based judges reaching 93.75% agreement with human annotations. Evaluating nine frontier models including Claude Sonnet and Opus 4, Computer-Use-Preview and GPT-5 yields a high average BGD rate of 80.8%. Prompting-based interventions lower BGD levels but substantial risk persists, indicating a need for stronger training- or inference-time interventions. Qualitative analysis surfaces three failure modes: execution-first bias (focusing on how to act over whether to act), thought-action disconnect, and request-primacy (justifying actions because the user asked).

#### Dark Patterns Meet GUI Agents: LLM Agent Susceptibility to Manipulative Interfaces and the Role of Human Oversight (Dark Patterns Meet GUI Agents) (2025-09)

A two-phase study comparing agents, humans, and human-AI teams against 16 dark pattern types. Phase 1's finding is the sharper one: agents often fail to recognize dark patterns, and even when they do recognize them, they prioritize task completion over protective action — so awareness alone does not produce safety. Phase 2 shows humans and agents fail *differently*: humans through cognitive shortcuts and habitual compliance, agents through procedural blind spots. Human oversight improved avoidance but brought its own costs in attentional tunneling and cognitive load, so neither party covers the other's gaps cleanly.

#### Characterizing Unintended Consequences in Human-GUI Agent Collaboration for Web Browsing (2025-05)

Drawing on social media analysis (221 posts) and semi-structured interviews (14 participants), this paper characterizes three unintended consequences of LLM-based GUI agents in web browsing across three perspectives: phenomena, influence and mitigation. Key phenomena include agents' deficient comprehension of instructions and planning of tasks, difficulty executing accurate GUI interactions and adapting to dynamic interfaces, generation of unreliable or misaligned outputs, and weak error handling and feedback processing. These manifest first as unanticipated actions and user frustration, then as privacy violations and security vulnerabilities, and further as eroded trust and wider ethical concerns. The analysis also documents user-initiated mitigations such as technical adjustments and manual oversight.
